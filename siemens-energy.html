View the Project Website ↗
Our Vision

Our team designed a 2050 system that collects unavoidable food waste from homes, schools, restaurants, grocery stores, apartments, and community spaces across Kitchener–Waterloo, then turns it into biogas, renewable natural gas, and electricity through anaerobic digestion. That energy helps power community resilience hubs — schools, shelters, community centres, and emergency warming/cooling centres — so they can keep running during outages, heat waves, and storms.

A website and app with QR codes are the public-facing part of the system. People scan codes on bins, posters, or cafeteria signs to learn what belongs in the bin, track their impact, find drop-off points, and see how much energy their community has helped create.

Why It Matters Locally
Local Context
  • Waterloo Region is projected to grow toward one million people by 2050 — more homes, more food businesses, more waste.
  • The Region already runs green cart organics collection, currently converted into compost; this project upgrades that into a smart circular-energy system.
  • Landfilled organic waste produces methane — landfills accounted for 17% of Canada's national methane emissions in 2023.
  • Anaerobic digestion breaks down organic material without oxygen, producing biogas for electricity, heat, or renewable fuel, plus digestate usable as fertilizer.
  • KW's existing wastewater plants (Kitchener, Waterloo, and Galt) already produce 13,000–22,200 m³ of methane-rich biogas per day — a proven foundation to build on.
How the System Works
1. Prevent & Rescue Food First

Edible food goes to food banks, shelters, student nutrition programs, or community fridges first. Only scraps and unavoidable spoiled food go to energy — feed people before making energy.

2. Collect Waste Smarter

QR codes on bins at schools, apartments, restaurants, libraries, and grocery stores let people log food waste, see what belongs in the bin, and earn points for their school or neighbourhood.

3. Use Data to Improve Collection

A dashboard predicts where food waste is highest and which routes reduce truck driving — the data-science layer of the system.

4. Convert Waste to Energy

Food waste goes to a regional anaerobic digestion facility or a co-digestion upgrade at existing wastewater infrastructure, producing biogas for electricity, heat, or renewable natural gas.

5. Power Resilience Hubs

Clean energy supports schools, shelters, libraries, and community centres, keeping lights, refrigeration, Wi-Fi, heating/cooling, and medical-device charging running during outages and emergencies.

What Makes It Unique
20 Resilience Hubs

Schools, shelters, libraries, and community centres that keep running during outages, heat waves, and winter storms.

Food Rescue Before Energy

Feed people → feed animals where appropriate → create energy → return nutrients to soil.

"Scraps to Watts" Tracker

Each QR code shows kilograms diverted, estimated clean energy generated, meals rescued, contamination rate, and a neighbourhood leaderboard.

Smart Bins

QR codes, fill-level indicators, colour-coded sorting guides, and contamination warnings — no expensive hardware required for the prototype.

Route Optimization

A simple map and dataset compare an old collection route against an optimized one to reduce truck driving.

Local Partnerships

Potential stakeholders include the Region of Waterloo, Sustainable Waterloo Region, schools, restaurants, grocery stores, food banks, local farms, wastewater facilities, and libraries.
